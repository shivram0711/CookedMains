What is Expected Value (EV) in UPSC Prelims Guessing?

Expected Value (EV) is the mathematical net mark expectation per question. In UPSC GS Paper-1, a correct answer awards +2.0 marks while an incorrect answer penalizes -0.66 marks. Eliminating two options (a 50:50 guess) yields a positive mathematical expectation of +0.67 marks [(0.5 × +2.0) - (0.5 × 0.66)]. Aspirants who attempt all 50:50 questions gain a statistical scoring edge over those who leave them unattempted.

The Mathematics of UPSC Prelims Negative Marking

Why 65–70 Attempts Almost Never Clear

Many beginners play "too safe" by attempting only 65 questions. If an aspirant gets 52 right and 13 wrong: Gross = 104, Negative = -8.6, Net = 95.4. Any slight dip in accuracy (e.g. 48 right, 17 wrong) drops the score to 84.8, missing the cutoff entirely.

The 50:50 Rule: Why You Must Guess

When you eliminate 2 options, probability is 50%. Across 20 such questions, expecting 10 right and 10 wrong gives: +20.0 - 6.6 = +13.4 Marks gained! UPSC Prelims rewards calculated, educated risk-taking.
