Question 1 (a) 10 Marks • 150 Words

"The doctrine of separation of powers is not rigidly followed in the Indian Constitution, yet checks and balances prevent authoritarianism. Critically analyse."

Introduction: The doctrine of separation of powers, formulated by French jurist Montesquieu, divides governance into Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary to preserve liberty.

Strong Anchor (+1.0M)

1. Non-Rigid Separation in India: The Indian Constitution establishes functional differentiation rather than water-tight compartments. Under Article 75(3), the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, fusing the Executive and Legislature.

Examiner Note: Clear identification of Art 75(3) collective responsibility. +1.0 / 1.5

Similarly, the President possesses legislative powers such as issuing ordinances under Article 123 during parliamentary recess. In turn, the Judiciary can invalidate unconstitutional executive actions.

Critical Gap (-1.0M)

2. Systemic Checks & Balances: Independent bodies like CAG and ECI ensure audit and free elections. However, recent trends show ordinances being promulgated without adequate post-session parliamentary debate.

Omitted Indira Gandhi (1975) Basic Structure doctrine on separation of powers. -1.0 Gap

Way Forward: Constitutional morality and revitalizing Parliamentary Standing Committees are vital to safeguard systemic equilibrium.
